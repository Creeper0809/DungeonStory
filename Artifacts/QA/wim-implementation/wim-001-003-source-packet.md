## Existing behaviour and compatibility faults

1. `CharacterApparelRuntime.TryPlanChange` validates actor, unique available physical item, catalog/component, anatomy/body form/points/size/openings, and builds a displaced-item plan. `TryCommitChange` routes the candidate to `apparel-equipped:<character>`, routes displaced items to `apparel-recovery-locker`, rolls routes back on failure, then changes the aggregate. `TryUnequip` routes back before removing the record. These are the required WIM-001 command and retention semantics, not a policy implementation.
2. `ApparelAvailabilityIndex` already filters layer, size, required points, tags/material tags, quality, contamination and optionally wet condition (`ApparelAvailabilityIndex.cs:115-209`), but no general selector calls it. It is the reusable candidate query; it must not be mistaken for an automatic policy.
3. Cold workwear is an intentional special path, not proof of general daily/work/spare changing. `TryAutoEquipForCold` orders available unlocked definitions and commits the first through the generic apparel command. Its selection has no `AllowsSpecies` call (repository reverse search for `.AllowsSpecies(` returned no live call), despite `EnvironmentalWorkwearSO.AllowsSpecies` being authored. Generic anatomy validation remains a separate check.
4. `ApparelWorkOrderRuntime` makes laundry/drying/repair recovery real: laundry clears contamination (and hand laundry leaves moisture at its existing authored value), drying clears moisture, and repair changes durability. The panel excludes equipped items, so the current recovery loop is wardrobe/inventory maintenance only. There is no current command that produces wear from clothing actually worn.
5. A usable weather source already exists: `SurvivalEnvironmentSnapshot.Weather` includes `Rain` (`SurvivalPrimitives.cs:145-190`), and work AI has weather pressure. No current apparel-condition writer consumes either it or an indoor/outdoor/worn exposure predicate. The P03 `rain exposure` requirement therefore needs an explicit mapping to the existing weather and location/exposure authority; it cannot be inferred from a global weather enum alone.

## Authorization and authoring decisions

1. **WIM-001 confirmed (2026-09-07):** automatic selection means a fit/purpose/condition criterion over available physical stock, not a reserved exact-instance slot. A player-directly-designated physical garment wins over that automatic choice. Laundry, breakage, or unavailability reselects a same-condition spare from stock; no spare is physically cloned or required to be a permanently fixed instance. Reuse the existing availability query and plan/commit/rollback; an unfit, reserved, inaccessible, or empty candidate set preserves the current garment and reports the reason. Re-evaluate only on purpose/input or inventory/apparel revision, never every tick. This confirms the policy shape, but does **not** approve WIM-002 protection numbers or WIM-003 condition rates.
2. **WIM-002 composition:** no existing definition declares how material 0--1 values convert to thermal units. The narrow initial candidate below is explicitly a recommendation, not an inferred approval; approve or revise it before asset/code authoring.
3. **WIM-003 accumulation:** no existing definition declares condition sources/rates. The narrow initial candidate below is explicitly a recommendation, not an inferred approval. General item-stack contamination remains unrelated unless a separately authored rule approves that link.

## 2026-09-07 follow-up: WIM-002/003 minimum authoring candidate (not approved)

The following is one deliberately narrow implementation-ready starting point. It changes no asset or source here. `W`, `H`, and water resistance always mean the existing `ApparelDerivedStats` values after material, tailoring coefficient, craftsmanship, durability band, condition, and opening deductions; raw textile values below are only their authored inputs. This keeps `0..1` material scales separate from Celsius and exposure multipliers.

### WIM-001 -- condition-triggered automatic change without a tick loop

Current laundry eligibility (`contamination > 0`) is a recovery-panel selector, **not** an automatic-change trigger. The following threshold names are a recommendation only; their values remain authoring approval items: contamination `C_hi/C_lo`, moisture `M_hi/M_lo`, and durability `D_lo/D_resume`, with each high trigger strictly beyond its lower re-entry value. An automatic selector runs only when a worn component crosses one of those edges (up through `C_hi`/`M_hi`, down through `D_lo`), on a purpose/direct-input change, or when the available candidate fingerprint materially changes. A candidate spare must be on the accepted side of `C_lo`, `M_lo`, and `D_resume`; consequently an item above a high threshold cannot swap straight back on the next condition tick.

The directly designated physical item remains the first normal candidate. If it reaches an approved recovery/breakage trigger, the policy may use the already-confirmed same-condition spare route, move the displaced physical item through the existing recovery destination, and preserve the designation's normal priority after it is again eligible. It must not override a direct designation merely because contamination became positive. A missing/reserved/inaccessible spare still follows the confirmed failure rule: retain the worn physical item and show the reason.

This needs no separate latch/store or framework. The player policy itself is persisted by its existing single owner: extend `CharacterApparelAggregate` and its current `DungeonCharacterEnvironmentSaveData.equippedApparel` capture/publish path, rather than creating a policy store. The crossing is known from the component's before/after values in the existing writer. `TrySetInstanceComponent` advances the global item-stack version, so raw `ItemStackVersion` alone must **not** be treated as a retry event or it would retry every condition write. The selector can compare the existing availability query's ordered physical-candidate fingerprint and `CharacterApparelRuntime` equip version, ignoring its own source-component write; a changed spare/reservation/recovery result then permits one new attempt. Any last-observed fingerprint is transient service memory only, not save data; restore may make one safe re-evaluation but creates no second persistent authority.

### WIM-002 -- one composition, no repeated base

For every currently equipped physical item with an apparel component, project its material once, then calculate `W_outfit = clamp01(sum(W_i))` and `H_outfit = clamp01(sum(H_i))`. Convert that pair **once** into one material thermal profile. For each item that has fixed `EnvironmentalWorkwearSO.Protection`, add that fixed profile once; do not copy either value into character/environment state. The existing `ThermalProtectionProfile.Add` then combines the single material profile with the per-item fixed profiles (minimum offsets add, exposure multipliers multiply):

| Derived material input | Recommended converted contribution | Unit / duplicate-count boundary |

| `W_outfit = clamp01(sum(W_i))` | comfort and safe minimum offset `-2.0 * W_outfit` degrees C; cold-exposure multiplier factor `1 - 0.15 * W_outfit` | The two outputs are respectively degrees C and a unitless multiplier. The complete outfit can contribute at most `-2 C` and `.85`, never one conversion per garment. |
| `H_outfit = clamp01(sum(H_i))` | comfort and safe maximum offset `+2.0 * H_outfit` degrees C; heat-exposure multiplier factor `1 - 0.15 * H_outfit` | Same single capped conversion in the heat direction. |
| Water/Airborne/Sterility/Durability | no thermal contribution in this thermal conversion | Water is consumed only by the WIM-003 rain candidate. `AirborneResistance` is explicitly distinct from WIM-002 heat relief and is not renamed “breathability”; its air-pollution/spore consumer remains a separate environmental connection. Durability affects `W`/`H` through the existing projector only. |

### WIM-003 -- actual sources, bounded writer, and recovery-compatible rates

Use the existing `CharacterEnvironmentUnityAdapter` environment path, which already sees each live character's environmental cell and the real work context set/cleared by `WorkTaskExecutor`. Its elapsed time must come from the injected existing `IGameClock`, not a local cadence constant. Query every currently equipped apparel component (`ICharacterApparelQuery`); mutate only its existing `ApparelInstanceState` via `IWorldItemStackRuntime.TrySetInstanceComponent`. Wardrobe, storage, and the unrelated general stack-contamination field are excluded. The persisted component remains the sole condition authority.

| Real source and condition | Recommended initial delta | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Equipped baseline | contamination `+.125` component point per game-hour; durability loss `+.01 * M` point per game-hour | Every worn apparel component changes; contamination is not copied to a general stack. |
| Real active exterior work context | additional contamination `+.1875 * E` point/game-hour; additional durability loss `+.03 * M` point/game-hour | `E` is the existing-layer/occupied-point exposure result below, not a new asset value. Use real `workContexts`, not a fabricated work event. |
| `Weather == Rain` or `Storm` **and** actual exterior cell | moisture `+1.00 * E * (1 - waterResistance)` point/game-hour; multiply by `1.5` for Storm | Rain affects exposed worn portions; it adds no invented mud/filth contamination. Expose the field's stored exterior flag; do not infer exterior from temperature/light. |
| Material scale `M` | `clamp(100 / TextileMaterialDefinitionSO.Durability, .80, 1.75)` | Uses the immutable material value, not derived durability, preventing a durability feedback loop. |

At runtime convert each delta with `rate * IGameClock.DeltaTime / GameCalendarRules.SecondsPerGameHour`; day assertions use `GameCalendarRules.SecondsPerDay`. No literal `7.5` or `180` belongs in the writer. At the current calendar authority those values resolve to 7.5 real seconds/game-hour and 180 real seconds/day, which is only the packet's arithmetic cross-check. Clamp moisture, contamination, and durability to the existing `0..100` component range. Do **not** impose a durability floor at 20: retain the actual below-20 value, where current repair remains unavailable. A `<20` crossing is a candidate WIM-001 same-condition-spare/recovery trigger; the displaced physical instance remains in its existing recovery/unequipped route with its unchanged condition. If no spare can be committed, preserve the worn instance and report the failure. No automatic deletion, dismantling, replacement creation, or new repair rule is proposed; the design-original “dismantling target” classification is an explicit later player/workflow decision, not an effect added here. For generic apparel durability, do not branch on `tool:hauling-harness`: inspect the physical stack's existing `ItemInstanceComponentIds.Durability` capability together with `DurableToolItemRules.TryGetMaximumDurability`. When that durable-tool component owns wear, WIM-003 must not debit `ApparelInstanceState.durability`; its existing durable-tool owner is the sole debit path. A stack without that capability remains an apparel-component candidate. Material thermal composition remains independent and still occurs once.

For a transparent rate check, an item worn for 24 game-hours with 8 active work-hours loses `(24*.01 + 8*.03) * M = .48*M` durability points/day: common wool `.686`, frost wool `.571`, frost linen `.738`, mire canvas `.522`, ember cotton `.828`, rune leather `.384`. ### 2026-09-07 six-resident full-apparel candidate -- condition cadence, not a WU claim

The representative, non-combat humanoid work outfit is five simultaneous physical instances: `apparel:lower-underwear` (Underwear/Pelvis), `apparel:undershirt` (Underwear/Torso), `apparel:work-shirt` (Inner), `apparel:farmer-workwear` (Outer), and `apparel:belt` (Accessory). The two underwear definitions occupy disjoint points despite their common layer. `Armor` is deliberately absent. Six residents therefore keep condition for **30** real apparel components; underwear, inner, outer, and accessory are all state writers, never an outer-only loop.

One narrow, unapproved candidate derives a binary external supplement without a new field: order existing `ApparelLayer` values and inspect existing `OccupiedPoints`; `E=1` when an item has any point not overlapped by a higher worn layer, otherwise `E=0`. In this outfit, farmer workwear and belt have an exposed point; work shirt and both underwear items are fully covered. Every component receives the worn baseline `+.125` contamination point/game-hour (`3/day`). Active exterior work adds `+.1875*E` point/hour (`+1.5/day` for the exposed twelve); rain adds `+1.00*E*(1-waterResistance)` moisture point/hour and Storm multiplies it by 1.5. This is overlap/non-exposure only: covered garments still accumulate worn contamination and their own durability state.

Set the one automatic change edge, pending approval, to `contamination >= 54 OR moisture >= 96`; a replacement spare must be clean/dry (`0/0`). At eight exterior work-hours/day the exposed twelve reach contamination 54 on day 12, while the covered eighteen reach it on day 18. With normal-quality, durability-band-4 mire canvas, the lowest representative water resistance is the belt's `.72*.62=.4464`; even Storm adds only `1.5*(1-.4464)*8=6.6432` moisture/day, so the contamination edge remains first at day 12. `contamination > 0` occurs on day 1 for all 30 and keeps its existing **manual laundry eligibility only**; it does not create an automatic swap or order.

| 36-day repeating condition schedule | Changed physical items | Hand orders / `requiredWork` | Drying orders / `requiredWork` | Order-progress total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Day 12 | exposed outer + accessory = 12 | 1 / 12 | 1 / 24 | 36 |
| Day 18 | covered inner + two underwear groups = 18 | 2 / 24 | 2 / 48 | 72 |
| Day 24 | exposed outer + accessory = 12 | 1 / 12 | 1 / 24 | 36 |
| Day 36 | all groups = 30 | 3 / 36 | 3 / 72 | 108 |
| **Total / daily average** | `72 / 36 = 2` garments/day | `7 / 84` | `7 / 168` | **252 / 36 = 7 `requiredWork`/day** |

The seven actual batches provide 84 item slots/36 days (`2.333/day`), below baseline 4.7's required `1.25*2 = 2.5/day`; a valid facility schedule must have one additional unused 12-item hand-laundry and drying window in that period (96 slots/36 days = 2.667/day). That is capacity headroom, not an empty work order or a second state system.

**Actual unit evidence / frozen labour conclusion:** `ApparelWorkOrderRuntime.CreateLaundry/CreateDrying` author `requiredWork` 12/24 and `ApplyWork` accepts that amount as order progress. But `ResearchFacilityOperationFallbackHandler.ApplyApparelOrder` runs only after a completed normal `Operate` cycle and calls `IApparelWorkOrderCommand.ApplyWork(order.requiredWork - order.completedWork)` directly; it does not call `WorkExecutionContext.ExecuteWorkAmount(order.requiredWork)`. The fallback is registered as the `Operate` completion handler. Therefore `252/36 = 7` is **order-progress units/day**, not demonstrated WU, and no source currently proves `requiredWork == ExecuteWorkAmount ==` the supplied 270-WU daily budget. The former 108/9/12-WU claims are withdrawn. Do not change current authored 12/24 values on this evidence; first measure the real `Operate` caller/cycle amount, worker performance multiplier, and 36-day scheduling trace.

`ApparelDefinitionSO` authors layer and occupied points but has no serialized exposed-area or contamination-coefficient field. The binary overlap test is consequently the only existing-data expression for this candidate; an arbitrary per-layer numeric table would need separate authoring and is not proposed.
